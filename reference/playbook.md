# The one-sentence reel playbook

Short, practical rules for reels that get watched to the end and get a comment. They are what this
skill follows by default. Nothing here is a promise of views; test, then keep what works for you.

## 1. The first second

- **The hook is the first line, seven words or fewer**, and it is already on screen in frame one.
- Make it about the viewer: a question they would answer yes to ("Don't want to be on camera?"),
  a claim they want to argue with ("You're not lazy. Your program is random."), or a specific
  number ("Why our sourdough takes 36 hours").
- No logo intro, no "hi guys", no title card. The brand sits small at the top the whole time.
- One accent word per page. Colour the word that carries the point.

## 2. The middle

- **One idea per reel.** If you have three points, you have three reels.
- **Something changes every two or three seconds:** a new headline page, a card, a tick, a number.
- **Every line names what is on screen.** If the line says "18 hours in the fridge", the card shows
  18 hours. Never talk over a picture of something else.
- Short sentences. About 2.6 words a second. If a line needs a breath in the middle, split it.
- Show proof instead of adjectives: a step, a price, a before/after, a real output.
- 15 to 30 seconds is the sweet spot for a single point.

## 3. The ending: one ask

- End with a single comment keyword in capitals and what they get: "Comment BREAD and I'll DM you
  tomorrow's bake list." Say it in the video and in the caption, once each, plainly.
- Pick a word people can type without thinking (one word, no spaces) and that tells you which
  reel they came from if you run several offers.
- Ask them to follow first if you will DM them; messages from accounts they don't follow land in
  their requests folder, where many never look.
- Avoid bait wording ("comment to unlock part 2", "share or it disappears"). Platforms push those
  down.

## 4. The comment-to-DM loop

1. Pin a comment that repeats the offer in one sentence.
2. In the first hour after posting, reply to every keyword comment with a short public reply
   ("Sent! Check your DMs") and send the DM. Early replies and DMs are signals that the post is
   worth showing to more people.
3. DM only people who asked. Never message first, never ask for personal details.
4. Keep one DM text per offer and paste it; personalise the first line if you can.
5. If the app says "Try again later", stop replying and liking for a day.

## 5. Trial reels: test hooks on strangers

Instagram's trial reels are shown to people who don't follow you first, and you decide later
whether to share them to your followers. That makes them the cheapest way to test hooks.

- Post 5 to 10 versions of the same offer with **different hooks and different opening scenes**.
  Keep the ending the same, so the only difference is the hook.
- Wait 48 to 72 hours, then compare in each reel's insights:
  - **views and how many were from non-followers** (reach),
  - **average watch time and the 3-second hold** (did the hook work?),
  - **comments per 1,000 views** (did the ask work? under 1 means it did not),
  - **shares and saves** (would they send it to someone?),
  - **follows** from the reel.
- Keep the top two hooks. Rewrite only the first line of the rest and test again.
- When one wins clearly, share it to your followers and make five more in the same angle.

## 6. Covers and captions

- The cover is the hook, big, on a clean background. The skill makes it from your first line.
- Caption: the hook in plain words, one or two sentences of value, then the ask, then 3 to 5
  specific hashtags. No wall of tags.
- Write the DM reply before you post, so you can answer in seconds.

## 7. Safe zones (1080 x 1920)

Instagram covers parts of the frame with its own buttons and text:

- keep 120 px clear on the left and right,
- nothing important below y = 1480 (username and caption),
- below the middle, stay left of x = 960 (like, comment, share buttons),
- the top 250 px holds the app header; the skill uses it only for the small brand line.

## 8. Honesty

- Only real numbers and claims. If you show made-up screens, label them "Sample data".
- Don't show other brands' logos, characters or music as if they were yours.
- If you add an AI voice, turn on the AI label when you post.
- Results vary. Say "our results, not a promise" if you show your own numbers.

## Checklist before posting

- [ ] Hook is 7 words or fewer and visible in frame one
- [ ] One idea, 15 to 30 seconds, something new every 2 to 3 seconds
- [ ] Every number is real (or labelled sample data)
- [ ] One keyword ask, in the video and the caption
- [ ] Pinned comment ready, DM reply ready
- [ ] Contact sheet checked: no overlaps, nothing in the bottom or right-side buttons
- [ ] Posted as a trial reel if you are still testing hooks
